# Wallet access counts cannot measure unique-person adoption

Question: What can NRB wallet counts establish, and what remains unknown?
Status: desk_review · Period: Source-specific periods stated with each claim
Geography: Nepal national

**Research artifact — human source and method review required.**

## Claims and evidence

### W1 · fact

The wallet access row reports 28,150,508 at mid-Sept 2026.

- supports: [Monthly Payment Systems Indicators: Bhadra 2083 (Mid-Sept 2026)](https://www.nrb.org.np/psd/payment-systems-indicators-of-2083-bhadra/); Page 1, A4, 2083 Bhadra; period Mid-Sept 2026; geography Nepal national; checked 2026-11-12.

Limitations: An access/account count; the source does not establish unique persons or activity.

### W2 · fact

The wallet usage row reports 51,494,949 monthly transactions for Bhadra 2083.

- supports: [Monthly Payment Systems Indicators: Bhadra 2083 (Mid-Sept 2026)](https://www.nrb.org.np/psd/payment-systems-indicators-of-2083-bhadra/); Page 1, B12, transaction column, 2083 Bhadra; period Mid Aug 2026–Mid Sept 2026; geography Nepal national; checked 2026-11-12.

Limitations: National descriptive evidence only; no locality or causal inference.

### W3 · inference

Access counts and monthly transaction counts measure different units and time bases. Neither establishes the number of distinct active people in a municipality.

Basis: W1, W2


Limitations: No deduplicated user or geographic breakdown is present.

### W4 · recommendation

Use a separately sourced, defined active-user measure for adoption research; preserve its population, period and deduplication method.

Basis: W1, W2


Limitations: National descriptive evidence only; no locality or causal inference.

## Study limitations

- No person-level data or local inference.
- Do not divide access counts by population and label the result unique-person adoption.

## Next actions

- Define active user before selecting a denominator.
- Request public aggregate methodology rather than private customer records.
